# Tanmay Bhat content playbook

Principles from Tanmay Bhat's interviews, talks and his own channel, rewritten as working rules.
Sources are Instagram clips (found via the fan page @tanmaythinks_) and full-length YouTube
talks. Each rule names its source id; `INDEX.md` links every id to the original.

## 1. Retention is the whole game

- Every format, long or short, lives or dies on watch time. Write for "will they reach the last
  second", not "is this good". (`2026-09-25_DdtfzTTNy2I_1`)
- Build to a payoff at the end. Trends and countdowns work because they withhold the answer
  until the close. In a demo or tutorial, say up front what the result will be, show the steps,
  and reveal the finished thing last: the "oven moment". (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Promise the payoff early, deliver it late. The opening line should make the viewer need the
  ending.
- Predictability rewards, surprise rewards more. Write every piece as "X expected, Y happens":
  name the expectation and the flip before scripting, and set up the context as fast as
  possible, because the sooner the viewer is settled the harder the flip lands.
  (`2026-09-28_Dd0_iCtBlAq_1`, `B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Whatever cures boredom today bores people tomorrow. Keep reinventing length, pacing and
  format before the audience tires of them. (`eQ-ljb2VFdU`, `B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Use dramatic irony: let the viewer see the outcome coming before the people on screen do, and
  build the whole video toward that moment. The wait itself holds them. (`IMTetzcc6AE`)
- Optimise completion rate, not raw watch time. Making videos longer inflates watch time while
  hiding weak retention. His own benchmark: about 70% on average, around 60% for a weak video,
  low-to-mid 70s for a strong one. (`hKGe6WbHpWY`)
- Cut to the creator's own laugh after a punchline. It works like a laugh track and makes the
  viewer feel they are watching with friends. (`hKGe6WbHpWY`)
- Keep the screen alive. Every few words something should change: a cut, a slow zoom, a music
  hit, a sound effect, an inserted meme. Put this in the editor's brief as a standing rule.
  (`4Any9xEl-qA`)
- Treat every scene of a long video as its own short, with its own hook and its own closing
  beat. A few great moments carrying filler no longer holds an audience. (`4Any9xEl-qA`)
- Viewers can tell when a video exists only to keep a schedule. If you are phoning it in,
  change the format rather than fill the slot. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- Teaching? Package the lesson like a short: a few quick points, music, text changing on screen,
  something fun around it. Dry material gives the brain nothing to enjoy. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- A short-form view count includes everyone who scrolled past. When the goal is clicks or
  sales, lean on long-form: ten straight minutes with you builds closeness and click-through.
  Use shorts to get found. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Prefer formats that bring a new audience with every episode (a new guest, the day's news), so
  each upload pulls in fresh viewers. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- The real win in short-form is the viewer who opens your profile and watches ten more. Build
  formats people can binge. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Followers matter less every year. Platforms keep showing you to people who have clicked on
  you a few times, so package every video to earn the next click, not just the follow.
  (`75tkthAzZog`)

## 2. Design for the share button

- Every video earns one of two shares; pick one and write toward it
  (`2026-10-02_Dd_dODBzpB6_1`):
  - **Send to a friend**: "this is so you / so us". Built on a shared identity. The sharpest
    version is "gifting an emotion": a near-universal situation (living with a flatmate) that
    the viewer sends to the exact person who lived it with them. (`6XEK0I9HUk8`) Choose the
    size of the group on purpose: a shared emotion reaches almost everyone, a personal detail
    ("only tall guys get this") reaches a few people very hard. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
  - **Post to my profile**: the sharer uses it to look a certain way. Give them a take that
    sets them apart while still feeling relatable. (`8DYIqJw5_bw`)
- For professional audiences, "post to profile" usually wins: make the viewer look smart for
  passing it on.

### Reliable share triggers (`6XEK0I9HUk8`, `8DYIqJw5_bw`, `B_Uh5V4xD4k`)

Pick one before scripting. With no clear trigger, default to warm and cheerful.

- **Topic of the day.** A ready-made opinion on what everyone is discussing gets forwarded as
  the viewer's own. Speed decides it: for a known event, draft posts for each likely outcome in
  advance and publish the moment it lands.
- **The opposite take.** Once a view becomes consensus, there is demand for a credible voice
  against it. A strong stance gets traction; fence-sitting does not.
- **Identity.** "Only people who X will get this" is close to a sure thing. Identity plus
  positivity is the strongest pair.
- **Warmth.** Hyper-positive, cute (dogs, babies), nostalgic, or riding a wave of affection for
  someone people already love. A break from a grim feed gets passed on.
- **Sensation.** Gossip or anything that breaks the everyday.

### Titles and thumbnails (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)

- Check that people care about the topic before reaching for title tricks; a clever paradox
  about something nobody cares about still gets nothing.
- Learn packaging from yourself: notice what made you click on a video, and why.

## 3. Starting a page from zero

1. Pick a narrow niche and a memorable page name. Find what is missing in it and shape yourself
   to fill that gap; a faceless upload with no voice disappears among thousands of identical
   ones, so put a personality on screen. (`eQ-ljb2VFdU`)
2. Do not post yet. Pull the top 10 videos from the biggest channels in that niche, and cluster
   them into the 5 or 6 recurring topics. Those titles are proven demand.
   (`2026-10-02_Dd_dODBzpB6_1`)
3. Start in short-form: it is cheap, so you get several attempts a day instead of one a week,
   and an early win keeps you going. Move the audience to long-form once you have one.
   (`75tkthAzZog`)
4. For the first 1,000 views, ask friends with real followings to put a good video on their
   stories, then let the platform take over. Who reshares matters more than how many.
   (`75tkthAzZog`)
5. Treat long-form as the anchor. A big YouTube audience tends to follow you to other
   platforms; a big Instagram audience rarely comes back the other way. (`8DYIqJw5_bw`)
6. Run "spikes and waves". About once a week, ship a spike (a collab, or a special you tease
   for days) to pull in new viewers; fill the gaps with steady routine uploads that keep them
   until the next spike. Measure fans who turn on notifications, not views alone. A new channel
   cannot afford one big swing a month; that pace is only for people who already have an
   audience. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)

## 4. Find your angle: "comedy plus X"

- Treat comedy (or any style) as the medium, not the content; the subject is what you carry
  inside it. Compete on your combination: style plus real curiosity. (`2026-09-23_DdooXuFSmLq_1`,
  `2026-10-04_DeE8mP3TQlu_1`)
- When a medium gets crowded, perspective beats volume. Jokes are everywhere now (memes,
  tweets); opinions and stories are scarce, and the stand-up that broke through was story-driven
  with a strong point of view. (`4Any9xEl-qA`, `hKGe6WbHpWY`)
- Look for what traditional media cannot or will not show, and make that. (`8DYIqJw5_bw`)
- List what everyone in your category does, then do the opposite: rude where the rest are
  polite, yesterday's stars where everyone casts today's. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- A crowded niche is still open if you break its format rules, for example long, film-like
  videos where everyone else makes the same short ones. A formula that is tired for one
  audience can feel new to another. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- Your audience follows you across formats (sketches, vlogs, reactions) as long as your core
  skill stays the same. When you move to a bigger stage, do not become a stiffer version of
  yourself. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- In a crowded category, the format can be the difference, not the opinion. His film reviews
  are a fast run of jump cuts listing the thoughts he had while watching. Give the category
  your own repeatable wrapper. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Over ten years, judge an angle by how it scales and how tired it makes you. A skill you can
  apply to any subject (making anything funny) lets you move between genres; an angle that
  needs travel or special access wears you out. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- A react or review format built on clips that already went viral has a quality floor: your
  only job is to add something on top and not be the boring part. (`IMTetzcc6AE`)
- Every field has choices that impress fellow practitioners but not the audience. Spot the ones
  aimed at peer approval and drop them when they do nothing for the viewer. (`nWuk46Ifl9E`)
- Avoid formats where the audience can do your job better. In a react video the comment section
  will out-joke the panel every time. (`4Any9xEl-qA`)

## 5. Reps beat talent

- Expect to throw most of it away. He puts it at roughly a thousand pages written a year for
  50 to 100 usable ones; the volume is how you get good. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Shorten the loop: make, publish, read the response, adjust, hundreds of times. Judgment comes
  from the number of loops, not years served. A feed posting several times a day learns more
  in a month than a channel posting twice a month does in a year; somewhere past the 150th
  attempt you can predict how a piece will do. (`75tkthAzZog`, `B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Take a wide range of work early (columns, TV, award shows, ads). That range becomes the
  fluency that later makes any format easy. (`c6eC0gF6dHQ`)
- Stuck in a slump? Pick work with a fast feedback loop at your current skill level, such as
  live-streaming, instead of a distant goal like "impact". (`c6eC0gF6dHQ`)
- Let data choose the area and angle to aim at, then let the creative run freely inside it.
  Numbers cannot write the idea. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Learn how a piece is constructed, not just one platform. Construction carries across TV, ads,
  YouTube and reels, which is what lets you move when the next medium arrives. Joke writing is
  the most portable version: it makes a job post or a live stream better too.
  (`75tkthAzZog`, `hKGe6WbHpWY`)
- Run the channel like a product team: before each upload ask what this audience wants next,
  double down on what works, and keep a few experiments running beside it. (`hKGe6WbHpWY`)
- When a video hits, turn it into a series on the same premise. Give a new series about three
  episodes; if none comes close to the original, drop it and try the next one. (`hKGe6WbHpWY`)

### Format succession (`IMTetzcc6AE`)

- Expect a winning format to wear out in about three months. Before it does, run a string of
  cheap tests of new formats, expect most to flop, and have the next one live as the current
  one fades.
- Build the next format from the mechanism of the last one, not a blank page: work out why the
  winner works (say, a panel-by-panel build to a punchline) and find a new subject with the
  same structure.
- Prefer topics that bring a new group of viewers with them; content about a country or a
  community tends to pull that group in as followers.
- A repeating data source (for example, the week's most shared memes) can become a fixed weekly
  series, and a fixed series can grow into a property of its own.
- A format that depends on the people or places around you dies when your life changes. Treat
  that as the cue to find the next one.

### Numbers to watch (`hKGe6WbHpWY`)

- Read the channel monthly, not video by video. The per-upload dashboard compares each video to
  the last ten and works like a stock ticker: built to make you anxious.
- Keep a short list: the monthly trend, comments per video, unique viewers.
- Comments are a leading signal. Months with more comments brought more new subscribers on his
  channel, so script moments that make people want to reply.
- Re-check format rules against how the platform works today. "Every video must pass 10
  minutes" became "cut everything" within a decade. (`4Any9xEl-qA`)
- Set the next concrete goal the day you hit one: first million-view video, then a sold-out
  show, then a format nobody in your market has tried. (`8DYIqJw5_bw`)

## 6. Content is an asymmetric bet

- A post, like a book or a first date, can return far more than it cost. Volume of good
  attempts is the strategy. (`2026-09-26_DdwCFZwxnXv_1`)
- Risky ideas are how you go viral, and some will embarrass you in public. Decide in advance to
  own those misses. (`9k8Gsb45o5Q`)
- Keep a proven format next to every risky one. When the risky piece flops, go straight back to
  what works and ship again; do not sit with the flop. (`9k8Gsb45o5Q`)
- Beware variable rewards. One breakout makes you chase it in every video after; judge a video
  on its own job. (`2026-10-07_DeMYoJMTdBW_1`)

## 7. Fit before money

- Enjoyment fit comes first, business fit follows. Creating only because it is the thing to do
  burns out. (`2026-09-24_DdrW_s0zCFP_1`)
- Keep the job while you start; quit only when content alone could carry you for about a year.
  His own switch took 3 to 4 years to match his old salary. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Do not plan on short-form ad revenue. Money comes from long-form ads and brand deals, priced
  on views, genre (finance pays more) and how premium you seem. Fewer deals a year raises the
  rate. (`75tkthAzZog`) Long-form ad income is bigger than most assume; what sets the rate per
  view is how long each view lasts and how many viewers come from high-paying regions or pay
  for Premium. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Education and finance creators already have a product (courses); entertainers do not, so
  build a business out of your craft skill, as a comedy writer moving into advertising and
  advising startups. Co-found with past collaborators who run operations; your one job stays
  growing the audience. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- If you build a business on your audience, a service business is an easier first step than a
  consumer brand. Partner with someone who knows the field, and remember the audience only feeds
  it while you keep finding new viewers. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Growing a page from 0 to 10,000 proves you can build distribution and sell; it beats a resume.
  (`2026-09-30_Dd6g0w2zUVp_1`, `2026-10-01_Dd9Rxv5TAzC_1`)

## 8. Team, partners and pitching

- Pitch by reading the work well and reading the room. Concede the line you sense will not
  survive; giving ground buys trust for the rest. (`2026-09-29_Dd3ynUGSRSJ_1`)
- Run small pods that each own one property (for example two editors and a producer). Do not
  make one person juggle several shows. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Know your partners. "Sharpshooters" stay quiet and then give one strong idea; "workhorses" give
  you range. Use each for what they do best. (`9k8Gsb45o5Q`)
- Work with the same collaborators through many rounds until you can tell an idea will land
  without the room laughing. (`9k8Gsb45o5Q`)
- Use a high-volume channel (a social feed posting several times a day) to test idea
  combinations before committing a bigger production. (`9k8Gsb45o5Q`)
- Share upside: when a sponsored piece pays, everyone on it gets a cut, and credit every team
  member by name and handle in each video. Many creators who faded did so after their team
  left. (`2026-10-02_Dd_3MbUTgWz_1`, `JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Generate wide, then narrow. Collect the best existing work in the category first, then have
  the team pitch around 20 ideas, shortlist about 5, develop those, and keep saying "no winner
  yet" until one clearly lands. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Keep a small circle of people with sharp taste and send them drafts constantly. Use them
  mainly to find what to cut; ignore critics who are performing cleverness. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- After weeks inside a script, remember the viewer sees it once, fresh. Do not cut the bits
  that made the room laugh the first time just because you are tired of them. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Test on a room that looks like the audience, and aim for broad approval of the taste, not
  unanimous agreement. (`c6eC0gF6dHQ`)
- Keep an "insight Bible": the team logs everyday observations by topic as they happen, so
  when a brief lands you already have raw material for ten ideas in a couple of hours. (AIB
  practice, `EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- When a client says no, ask exactly why. A precise reason becomes a guardrail for the next
  round; vague no's are what stall projects. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- Working up close with a bigger creator shortens the climb a lot. (`75tkthAzZog`)

## 9. Content for brands

- Content pays back in ways a campaign sheet misses: it attracts hires and makes staff proud of
  where they work. Do not judge it on direct return alone. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Start with short video for fast feedback, and run content as always-on, not a one-off
  campaign. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Keep one in-house producer who owns the brand's taste and outsource the rest. The best person
  for that seat has built an audience themselves. (`75tkthAzZog`)
- Aim for great work, not for never making bad work. Test: could someone watch a year of your
  ads back to back and enjoy it, even for a dull product? (`9k8Gsb45o5Q`)
- Meeting expectations builds the brand; beating them makes the content. Keep one recognisable
  style, but change the execution often, because audiences tire of things faster now.
  (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Treat a hard length cap as fixed and assume the first draft says too much. Cut through at
  least two rounds before pitching; saying less leaves the audience room to fill in.
  (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Write to the assets you actually have. A concept built on a specific celebrity or prop dies
  without them, so confirm access first. (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)
- Utility alone is forgettable. Useful and beautiful is what people get attached to, so treat
  polish as loyalty-building, not decoration. (`nWuk46Ifl9E`)
- Celebrity endorsements work mostly through legitimacy: viewers read "this brand can afford
  them" as a sign it is trustworthy and here to stay. Booking a big creator sends the same
  signal. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Work content-first: find what is interesting, then where the brand fits. For a campaign that
  runs for weeks, build a repeatable gag that makes people ask "who's next", bold enough to be
  talked about and catchy enough to survive heavy repeats. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- Separate campaigns that move a number now from brand building, and do not judge one campaign
  on every metric. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- A creator mentality means trying to go viral again and again for years, not one stunt.
  Established brands should build an in-house creator team; startups can bring a creator in
  early. Hire marketers who make their own content and sometimes fail at it. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- When pitching, name the knock-on value beyond views: easier hiring, proud employees, easier
  sales. The best clients act as collaborators and have a founder who has said a miss is fine.
  (`B_Uh5V4xD4k`)

## 10. A career that outlasts the algorithm

- Viewers binge you for a few months and then move on, and creators arrive in batches. Plan
  for a steady inflow of new viewers, and expect to reinvent yourself every few years or the
  next batch replaces you. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Brand deals are "high rent": you are renting out your time. While still growing, start
  income streams two, three and four (affiliate, consulting, merch, a business) and try them
  all, since you cannot predict what your audience will pay for. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Build income your opinions cannot touch before taking strong public stands. Brand money is
  first to leave when the comments turn; financial security buys the freedom to speak.
  (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- If growth stalls, a marketing job is a ready exit: brand marketing is mostly judging what will
  work, and you have practised that through more attempts than most marketers. (`JjDjDvNgkFo`)
- Track views per subscriber as your relevance signal; it drifts down as a channel ages. Measure
  each new push against your own best results, not your subscriber count. (`IMTetzcc6AE`)
- When long-time fans ask for the old style back but the new one is doing better, follow the
  numbers. Annoying some of them is the price of not staying in a format that is quietly dying.
  (`IMTetzcc6AE`)
- Run the career as a "marathon of sprints": push hard on one bet, rest properly, then start the
  next sprint fresh, instead of grinding at one pace until you burn out. (`IMTetzcc6AE`)
- One viral clip does not make paying fans. People who watch a single video forget you; only
  viewers who watch many of your videos become a following that buys tickets. (`hKGe6WbHpWY`)
- Treat the audience as step one, not the career. Plan as if you had to stop posting in two
  years, and use today's reach to build things no platform can switch off. Plan for the decline
  while still growing. (`c6eC0gF6dHQ`)
- Read hostile comments as a preview of how critics will come at you once you are bigger, and
  prepare. The precise criticism that stings is the useful kind. (`c6eC0gF6dHQ`)
- More followers is not the only route to more brand money. Creators who give real value earn
  more per fan than pure entertainers, whose pull fades; look for deals where the audience
  gains too. (`c6eC0gF6dHQ`)
- Do not live on brand money alone. Earn directly from fans (a paid inner circle, where a few
  thousand monthly members is a real income) or from utility, and make paying frictionless
  instead of stitching it together from stories, chat groups and meeting links. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)
- Fans who already help each other in your comments are a community waiting for a home. Give
  them one, with topic channels, live voice rooms and good moderators; your first power users
  come from that group. (`EVu2Uxu_8iU`)

## Review checklist (run on every script)

- [ ] Does line one create a question only the ending answers?
- [ ] Is the best item or reveal last?
- [ ] Is there one twist that beats the obvious expectation?
- [ ] Does something change on screen every few seconds?
- [ ] Which share is this built for: send-to-friend or post-to-profile? Which trigger does it use?
- [ ] Is this a proven topic in the niche, or a guess?
- [ ] What is the angle: whose real curiosity or point of view is showing?
- [ ] Could the comment section do this better than we can?

## Rules of use

- Borrow the mechanics, not the man. Do not imitate his voice, reuse his lines or jokes, or
  write as if he said something he did not.
- Never imply Tanmay Bhat endorses, sponsors or reviewed the content being made.
- When attributing an idea to him, link the original from `INDEX.md`. If you have not checked
  the source, say "according to a talk by Tanmay Bhat", not a direct quote.
